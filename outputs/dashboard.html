How it was made

Five steps, all reproducible from the src/ package.

Acquire

Sentinel-1 GRD scenes from 2026-08-16 (pre) and 2026-08-28 (post), the Copernicus GLO-30 DEM, and an OpenStreetMap snapshot of 2026-07-27 taken before the flood.

Align

SNAP preprocessing (orbit file, border and thermal-noise removal, sigma0 calibration, range-Doppler terrain correction at 10 m), then linear-power averaging to a common 30 m grid in UTM 45N (EPSG:32645).

Detect

Lee speckle filter, then the mean of the VV and VH change (post minus pre, dB). Threshold at the 5th percentile of that change, clipped to between −3.5 and −1.5 dB; keep flat land (slope under 12°) outside permanent water; remove patches under 20 pixels.

Impact

Intersect the mask with buildings, roads and settlements; find settlements disconnected from the road graph once flooded segments are removed.

Validate

Compare the mask with EMSR927 damage points against a random mask of the same size on flat pixels, and check the VV change along the river against shifted-river controls.

Limitations

The post scene is two days after the event; a flash flood in a narrow gorge may already have drained.
30 m pixels mix narrow channels and smear small structures, so building counts are indicative.
EMSR927 has no flood-extent layer, so validation against damage points is proxy only.
The detector threshold is clipped to −3.5…−1.5 dB, and the mask is selected from noise-level change.
The OpenStreetMap snapshot of 2026-07-27 predates the flood; later edits are not reflected, and rural Rasuwa is under-mapped, so missing buildings undercount damage and missing tracks can overstate cut-off.
Cut-off detection samples one midpoint per road segment and ignores footpaths.
Steep terrain (slope 12° or more) is masked out, so debris flows on slopes are not captured.
